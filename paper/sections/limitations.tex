\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The results support claims about recall over fixed histories under the reported evaluation conditions. The following limits prevent a broader conclusion about the value of generative memory writing.

\paragraph{Incomplete answer evaluations.} The matched LoCoMo test comparison covers only 222 of 1,155 questions. On those questions, our store scores 82.0 against Mem0's 85.6, a difference of $-$3.6 [$-$7.1, $-$0.5]. The subset oversamples hard categories. The pooled 607-question result also includes 385 development questions used to tune the ranker, so its aggregate tie does not establish a tie on the full test split. Our \lmes{} answer run covers 319 of 400 test questions, and the BEAM run covers 652 of 700. The remaining questions were not evaluated.

\paragraph{Selection on scored questions.} Some retrieval results include questions used to choose the configuration. The \lmes{} comparison on all 470 questions includes scored questions from the 150-question development split used for tuning. Earlier LoCoMo retrievers 5a and 5r were selected on all questions before the split existed. On Proced-Mem, configuration selection and evaluation use labels from the same 40 queries, and the judge can move a fixed ranking by up to 0.08. The Proced-Mem result therefore does not measure transfer to unseen queries.

\paragraph{Recall gains without answer measurements.} The two later search changes were selected on 383 development questions, including 74 multi-hop questions. Removing speaker names from the first stage recovers six development multi-hop questions. The recall improvement also holds on a frozen test ranking, but no reader was run on it. Improved retrieval therefore does not yet demonstrate improved answers. The token-matched LoCoMo run and the \lmes{} run with the user-turn rule were registered but never started (Section~\ref{sec:pending}); the same-judge comparison was run later, with one Claude judge scoring both systems' existing answers on LoCoMo and BEAM (Sections~\ref{sec:locomo} and \ref{sec:beam}).

\paragraph{Reader and judge dependence.} Only the main LoCoMo comparison shares Mem0's reader, judge and both prompts. On \lmes{} and BEAM the reader is shared, but our benchmark-specific judges differ from Mem0's GPT-5 judge and prompts. These scores cannot rank the two systems. Re-judging both systems' BEAM answers with one model lowers both pass rates by 6 to 10 points and reverses their order while keeping them within 2.6 points. That band is set by the judge as much as by the memory. Our own experiments move one retriever by three points by changing the reader alone, so comparisons across readers also confound the memory with the answer model.

\paragraph{Limits of published comparisons.} We did not run Mem0's pipeline ourselves; its scores come from published per-question files, which differ from its site figures by about a point on both chat benchmarks. Our \lmes{} retrieval comparison also depends on assumptions about EdgeMem that we have not confirmed with its authors: we interpret its R and N metrics as \rall{k} and \nany{k}, and assume its index includes both user and assistant turns. Its per-question outputs are unavailable. The margins therefore treat published results as unrelated samples and do not account for our tuning on part of the scored set. We ran no model-built memory with the same encoders as ours. The published retrieval comparisons therefore mix storage design with encoder choice. On LoCoMo, no published memory reports turn recall in the same form; the 16.3-point gain [+13.8, +19.2] is over our own flat retrieval baseline.

\paragraph{Benchmark defects and uncertainty.} Audits report that 6.4 to 7.0 percent of LoCoMo answer keys are wrong, and at least one evidence label in our miss analysis points to an unrelated turn. The LoCoMo test split contains only seven conversations, limiting the precision of conversation-cluster intervals. These issues affect both measured accuracy and interpretations of individual retrieval failures.

\paragraph{Resource tradeoffs.} Our 100-turn GPT-5 \lmes{} configuration reads 19,125 tokens per question against Mem0's 6,787. The proposed user-turn rule fits Mem0's budget and retains every gold turn for 97.9 percent of development questions, 2 points below the 100-turn setting, but its answer accuracy is unmeasured. On LoCoMo, search takes 1.05 s for 100 candidates, 3.16 s for 300 and 1.25 s with the cascade. These laptop timings are numerically above the 0.200 s 95th-percentile latency in Mem0's 2025 paper, but the hardware and timing summaries differ (Section~\ref{sec:cost}).

\paragraph{Scope.} A verbatim store preserves stated evidence but does not add facts absent from the history. Six of the 27 questions Mem0 answers and we miss require world knowledge not stated in a turn or have an evidence label that points elsewhere. The evaluations cover fact recall over fixed histories; they do not measure memory updates, forgetting or agent task success.
