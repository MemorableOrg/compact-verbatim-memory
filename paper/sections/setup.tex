\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

We evaluate conversational recall on LoCoMo, \lmes{} and BEAM, and procedural retrieval on Proced-Mem. The reader generates an answer from retrieved evidence; the judge grades that answer. Our main LoCoMo comparison with Mem0 shares the reader, judge and both prompts. The \lmes{} and BEAM comparisons share the GPT-5 reader but use different judges, so their answer scores do not establish a ranking between the systems. We specify the data splits, input budgets and evaluation conditions below.

\paragraph{LoCoMo.}
LoCoMo contains ten two-person conversations \citep{maharana2024locomo}. We score the 1,540 questions outside the adversarial category. Conversations 26, 30 and 41 form the development set of 385 questions; the other seven form the test set of 1,155. Every test accuracy therefore refers to these 1,155 questions or an explicitly stated subset. The matched comparison uses GPT-5 as both reader and judge, with Mem0's current answer and judge prompts copied from its evaluation repository \citep{mem0benchmarks2026}. Mem0's scores come from the per-question results in that repository. Secondary evaluations use Mem0's 2025 judge prompt on gpt-4o-mini \citep{chhikara2025mem0} and the reader named in each table. They provide context rather than a matched comparison with Mem0's current release.

Retrieval evaluations require usable evidence labels. The ranker study includes 383 development and 1,152 test questions; the earlier study of retrievers 5a and 5r includes 383 and 1,153. All-gold recall at $k$ is the share of questions for which every gold turn is among the top $k$ retrieved turns. A gold turn is one the benchmark labels as evidence for the answer.

\paragraph{\lmes{}.}
We use the cleaned release of 500 questions \citep{wu2025longmemeval}. Each question has a history of about 115k tokens across 48 sessions. Question ids are sorted by SHA-256 to define the splits.

Answer evaluations use the first 100 questions for development and the remaining 400 for test. GPT-5 reads the top 100 turns under Mem0's current answer prompt. We grade answers with the official judge, gpt-4o-2024-08-06, using its per-type prompts. Mem0's published results instead use GPT-5 as judge with its own prompt. The reader is shared, but the judge is not. A secondary evaluation uses Claude Sonnet 5.5 over 40 turns and the official prompt on all 500 questions.

The ranker retrieval study uses a separate split: 150 development questions and 350 test questions. Excluding abstention questions leaves 331 scored test questions. The earlier 5a/5r retrieval study used the 100/400 split, with 375 scored test questions (Appendix~\ref{app:retrieval}). Session-level \rall{k} is the share of questions for which every gold session is among the top $k$ retrieved sessions.

\paragraph{BEAM.}
BEAM contains conversations of 128k to 10M tokens, with 20 questions per conversation covering ten abilities \citep{beam2026}. We use the 1M-token tier: 35 conversations and 700 questions. GPT-5 first reads the top 20 turns and receives the top 40 if it answers NOT FOUND. BEAM's judge prompt on gpt-4.1-mini scores each rubric item as 0, 0.5 or 1. A question passes when its mean item score is at least 0.5, following Mem0's rule. Mem0's published answers are judged by GPT-5 with its own rubric prompt. No configuration was selected on BEAM; our results use a single run of the frozen configuration.

\paragraph{Proced-Mem.}
Proced-Mem has 40 queries over a bank of agent trajectories \citep{procedmem}. Each query returns ten trajectories. A gpt-5 judge at low effort scores each trajectory from 1 to 10, with scores of at least 6 counted as relevant. We use the repository's runner unmodified and report the mean of three judge passes because a single pass can move a fixed ranking by up to 0.08. Configuration selection and evaluation use the same 40 queries; the benchmark has no held-out split.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
Before each run, we registered its model, settings, memory-file hash and numbered predictions with ranges in a protocol file. Scripts read the result files and mark each prediction as held or missed. Appendix~\ref{app:predictions} lists the registrations and outcomes.

For the main conversation ranker, settings were selected on development data before the frozen test evaluation. Retrieval scores were reproduced on reruns from an empty cache. The two later search changes in Section~\ref{sec:search} were also selected using development labels, then ranked once on the test split with their settings frozen. Earlier retrievers selected on scored questions are marked as tuned below. Budget-stopped runs are reported only on completed questions; unstarted runs remain listed with their predictions in Section~\ref{sec:pending}.

\paragraph{Statistics.}
Unless stated otherwise, bracketed ranges are 95 percent intervals and differences in accuracy or recall are percentage points. We use Wilson score intervals for proportions of yes-or-no outcomes. LoCoMo accuracy comparisons use 2,000 bootstrap draws that resample whole conversations, preserving dependence among questions from the same conversation. Ranker comparisons use paired resampling so both systems are scored on the same sampled questions; captions specify where the resampling unit is a conversation rather than a question. Comparisons with published retrieval results use the independent-row margins stated in Section~\ref{sec:retrieval}.

\paragraph{Published and tuned results.}
Transcribed results carry the mark \pub{} and receive an interval only when their underlying count is known. The mark \tun{} identifies results whose retriever was selected or tuned on questions also included in the reported score. This applies to the earlier 5a/5r LoCoMo retrievers, selected before a split existed; the \lmes{} results on all 470 questions, including scored questions from the 150-question development split used for tuning; and the pooled 607-question LoCoMo comparison, including 385 development questions. Proced-Mem also carries this qualification because selection and evaluation share the same queries. In Table~\ref{tab:headline}, intervals are conversation-cluster bootstraps on LoCoMo and Wald margins between independent rows on \lmes{} retrieval. The published \lmes{} retrieval values are read as \rall{k}; that reading is inferred from the published tables. The two best-published accuracy rows carry a different reader and judge from ours. The search-time row sets our laptop median against two published figures: the mean latency Mem0's result file logs on its hosted service, which includes network time, and the 95th-percentile time Mem0's 2025 paper reports on its own hardware for an earlier release. Our 1.05 s is below the first and above the second. Tokens in that table are the reader's input per question.
